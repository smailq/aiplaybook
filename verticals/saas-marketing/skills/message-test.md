# Message test

A rubric for testing a positioning or messaging hypothesis honestly, so the book improves on evidence rather than opinion. Every message change should ladder up to the north-star metric and state how it moves it.

## When to use

- Whenever you propose a new tagline, hero line, audience angle, or channel message.
- Before recommending a human run a campaign, so there is a pre-registered bar for success.
- When a customer reports results and you need to judge whether the hypothesis held.

## Write the experiment card (before running)

1. **Hypothesis.** One sentence: "For [segment], [message/angle] will [effect] because [reason]."
2. **Segment and channel.** Exactly who sees it and where.
3. **The asset.** The concrete copy or variant to run, marked as a draft for human review. Give both the control and the variant.
4. **Metric and threshold.** The single metric that decides it (tie it to the north star), and the pre-registered win/lose threshold and sample/duration - decided *before* the run. Default to skepticism.
5. **Cost and risk.** Effort to run and anything irreversible or outward-facing (that stays a human decision - present it, do not perform it).

## After the run

6. **Judge honestly.** Compare the result to the pre-registered threshold. A flat or refuting result recorded honestly is worth more than an optimistic guess.
7. **Decide.** Double down, iterate, or kill - and say why.
8. **Revise the book.** Fold the outcome into the relevant section and the evidence log: update the messaging that tested well, retire what did not, and adjust the segment/channel beliefs and their confidence.

## Output

An experiment card added to the book (hypothesis, segment, channel, asset, threshold, and - after the run - result and the decision it drove), plus an evidence-log entry. Keep it traceable: every belief pairs with the result that backs it.
