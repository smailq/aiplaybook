# Competitor scan

A repeatable routine for building the outside view: who else solves this job, how they position, where they show up, and what that implies for our playbook. Run it at the start of a positioning or channel cycle, and re-run it when the market shifts.

## When to use

- Before writing or revising the Positioning chapter.
- When choosing which channel or message angle to test next.
- When a customer reports a competitor came up in a sales or churn conversation.

## Steps

1. **Frame the job.** State the specific job-to-be-done in one sentence (from the product brief and onboarding answers), not the product category. Competitors are whoever a buyer would consider for that job, including "do nothing" and manual workarounds.
2. **Gather 4-8 alternatives.** Use the web. Prefer primary sources: their own site, pricing page, docs, changelog, and recent posts. Note the date of each source.
3. **For each, capture:** the one-line promise (their hero copy), the audience they lead with, the top 3 claimed differentiators, pricing model and rough price, the channels they visibly invest in, and any proof they show (logos, numbers, reviews).
4. **Find the gaps.** Where is everyone saying the same thing (a crowded claim to avoid)? What real difference are we the only ones able to claim? Which audience is underserved by the incumbents?
5. **Turn it into implications, not a report.** Write 3-5 concrete implications for our book: a sharper positioning angle, a message to test, a channel that looks reachable, a claim to avoid because it is table stakes.
6. **Record evidence.** Add each implication to the evidence log paired with the source that backs it and a confidence level. Cite what you relied on.

## Output

Update or draft the relevant book section (usually Positioning or the channel roadmap) with the implications, marked as draft for human review, and append the sources to the evidence log. Lead with the decision the scan drives, not a survey of competitors.
